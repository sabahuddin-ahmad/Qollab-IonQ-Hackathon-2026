# Alt text for the figures

Generated by `scripts/make_result_figures.py`. Use these as the image descriptions on the project page.

**benchmark.png**

Dot plot of forecast error for nine models in exact, noiseless simulation, best at the top. The linear models HARX 0.1030 and ARMAX 0.1070 are lowest. QR1, the quantum reservoir, is third at 0.1102, with a range over 20 seeds from 0.1056 to 0.1133. Below it are HAR 0.1154, the classical reservoir RC 0.1171, AR3 0.1180, the classical reservoir RCX 0.1224, AR1 0.1302 and persistence 0.1433.

**complexity.png**

Two panels comparing HARX and QR1. Left, paired bars with counts: trained weights 14 for HARX and 10 for QR1; input values per forecast 13 and 21; fixed random parameters 0 and 45; qubits on IonQ 0 and 24; two-qubit gates 0 and 270. Right, time for one forecast on a log scale: HARX fit and forecast 0.27 ms; QR1 exact simulation 2.9 ms; QR1 noisy simulation 0.55 s; QR1 on IonQ's simulator 34 s at 100 shots and 163 s at 500 shots.

**scaling.png**

Three line charts against the number of input features, from 1 to 40, computed from the model definitions. First: trained weights grow linearly for both models, features plus 1 for HARX and features plus 3 for QR1. Second: QR1's two-qubit gates per circuit grow with the square of the qubit count, from 270 at 7 features to about 5,400 at 40; HARX uses none. A marker shows that more than 8 features does not fit a 29-qubit simulator without reset. Third, log scale: memory to simulate one QR1 circuit grows exponentially, passing a 16 GB laptop after 11 features with noise and 26 without, while HARX's training table stays under a megabyte. Each added feature quadruples the first and doubles the second.

**scaling_qubits.png**

Four panels on how the cost of QR1 grows. Top left: two-qubit gates per circuit rise with the square of the qubit count, 270 at 10 qubits and 2,610 at 30. Top right, log scale: measured laptop time to simulate one noisy circuit rises about fivefold per added qubit, 0.53 seconds at 10 qubits and 62 seconds at 13, extrapolated to about 1.8 hours at 16. Bottom left: without reset the circuit needs 7 qubits per month of memory plus 3, so 24 for three months and 31 for four, above the 29-qubit IonQ simulator limit; with reset it stays at 10. Bottom right: QR1 forecast error falls as shots increase, from 0.1168 at 256 shots to 0.1091 with no shot noise, and never reaches HARX at 0.1030.

**actual_vs_predicted.png**

Two stacked time series from 1997 to 2017 sharing one scale. Each shows realized monthly log volatility as a solid line and a one-month-ahead forecast as a dashed line: QR1 with coupling seed 0 on top (MSE 0.1098), HARX below (MSE 0.1030). Both forecasts track the slow rises and falls of volatility and are smoother than the realized series. Both miss sudden spikes, most visibly in late 2008; the largest QR1 miss is September 2008.

**actual_vs_predicted_noisy.png**

Three stacked time series from 1997 to 2017 sharing one scale, each showing realized log volatility as a solid line and a QR1 forecast as a dashed line, all simulated with 1.6 percent gate error. Top: 1024 shots with a noise-matched readout tracks the realized series closely (MSE 0.1110). Middle: 256 shots with a noise-matched readout tracks it with more month-to-month jitter (MSE 0.1274). Bottom: 1024 shots with a readout trained on a noiseless simulation has the right shape but sits 0.43 below the realized series on average (MSE 0.4006).

**shots_noise.png**

Line chart of QR1 forecast error against gate error from 0 to 3 percent, one line per shot count. With exact values the error stays near 0.109 at every noise level. At 1024 shots it rises from 0.1127 to 0.1171; at 256 shots from 0.1168 to 0.1382. Reference lines mark RCX 0.1224, HAR 0.1154 and HARX 0.1030: the 256-shot line rises above HAR at every noise level and above RCX beyond about 1 percent, while the 1024-shot line stays below RCX throughout. No line reaches HARX. A shaded band marks 1.6 to 2 percent, where IonQ's simulator sits.

**transfer.png**

Line chart on a log scale comparing two readouts at 1024 shots as gate error grows from 0 to 3 percent. Trained under the same noise, the error stays between 0.113 and 0.117. Trained on a noiseless simulation, it rises from 0.16 to 0.47 at 1 percent and 1.14 at 3 percent, far above persistence at 0.1433. Individual seeds vary widely for the noiseless-trained readout.

**ionq_calibration.png**

Per-qubit comparison for one circuit on ten output qubits: ideal values, a local depolarizing model at 1.6% per gate, and IonQ's Forte 1 simulator at 500 shots. IonQ's values are smaller in magnitude than ideal on every qubit and lie close to the local model. Overall IonQ keeps 0.65 of the ideal signal and the model 0.68; the three memory qubits lose the most, keeping about 0.4.
